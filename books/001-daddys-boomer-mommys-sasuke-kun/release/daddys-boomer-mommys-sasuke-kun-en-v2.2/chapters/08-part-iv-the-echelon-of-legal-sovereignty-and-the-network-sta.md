* **The grinding-down of the small-business benefit: the Passive Income Grind — section 125(5.1) ITA:**
  * *Primary sources [L1/L2 Spec]:* the *ITA*, section 125(5.1), section 129; CRA Income Tax Folio S7-F1-C11.
  * **The breach mechanics:** A CCPC is entitled to the Small Business Deduction (SBD) — a 9% federal rate (about 12.2% with the province in Ontario) on the first $500,000 of active income.
  * **The guillotine blade:** If the corporation accumulates passive investment income (dividends, interest, crypto, real-estate rent — AAII), then **every $1 of passive income above $50,000 cuts the active-income benefit limit by $5**!
  * At **$150,000 of annual passive income**, the right to the 12.2% preferential rate is fully annulled (a $0 limit). All the corporation's active income flies to the base rate of **26.5%–31%** (a $71,500/year tax hit on flat ground)!
  * The passive income inside the CCPC itself is taxed at the punitive bracketed rate of **~50.2%** (the RDTOH mechanism, with partial refund only on distribution of taxable dividends outward).

* **The Form T1135 trap (Foreign Income Verification Statement) — section 233.3 ITA:**
  * Holding foreign assets (Interactive Brokers accounts, crypto on foreign exchanges, US stocks, overseas real estate) with an aggregate cost above **CAD 100,000** at any moment of the year?
  * You must file the annual Form T1135.
  * **The penalty valve:** Late filing — **$25 a day, up to $2,500 a year** per delinquent year (section 162(7)). On gross negligence (section 163(2.4)) — **$500 a month for up to 24 months ($12,000)** plus draconian interest.

* **The Departure Tax — section 128.1 ITA (the Canadian exit tax):**
  * Leaving Canada = a deemed disposition of all assets at fair market value on the day preceding the loss of residency.
  * **[Level 1 correction:]** The Budget 2024 proposal to raise the capital-gains inclusion rate from 1/2 to **2/3 (66.7%) never became law**: its entry into force was first deferred to January 1, 2026, and on **March 21, 2025, the government formally cancelled the increase**. The inclusion rate remains **50%** for individuals, trusts, and corporations across the entire 2024–2026 horizon. The Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption (LCGE) was raised to CAD 1.25M in 2024, indexed to CAD 1.275M in 2026. The exit tax remains a deemed disposition — it just didn't get a third heavier by decree.

> 🔧 **Engineer-to-human translation (Bob's Rule for Canada):**
> * **The essence:** In Canada, family contracting through a pocket IT-CCPC is dead: issue dividends to a wife who isn't sitting in the repos 20 hours a week — take the 53.5% TOSI hit. And if you start accumulating money inside the company and investing it in stocks — you lose the 12% rate and catch a 50% tax on the passive income.
> * **Where the mine is buried:** Form T1135. Forgot to declare a foreign brokerage account or a cold crypto wallet with $100k — the CRA will issue a $2,500–$12,000 fine without a conversation.
> * **The action:** Keep the personal portfolio separate from the CCPC. With assets above CAD 100k outside Canada — a calendar reminder for the T1135 filing, synced with your personal T1 return.

###### **3.3. Singapore — the demolition of the "tax-free Asia" myth**

Singapore is sold as a zero-tax paradise: 0% on capital gains, territorial taxation, English law. But in 2024–2026 the screws got tightened here per EU and OECD standards.

* **Section 10L of the Income Tax Act 1947 — the end of tax-free sales of foreign assets:**
  * *Primary sources [L1/L2 Spec]:* the *Singapore Income Tax Act 1947*, section 10L (in force from January 1, 2024); the IRAS e-Tax Guide *"Tax Treatment of Gains or Losses from the Sale of Foreign Assets"* (updated June 2024 / June 2025).
  * **The breach mechanics:** Historically, the sale of a foreign business, a startup stake, stocks, or overseas real estate was not taxed in Singapore.
  * **The new reality of section 10L:** Capital gains from the disposal of any foreign assets (stocks, real estate, foreign IP), received in Singapore (remitted into Singapore) by a company within a group with a cross-border presence, **are treated as ordinary income and taxed at the 17% corporate rate** — UNLESS the company proves sufficient **Economic Substance** in Singapore!
  * **What IRAS requires for the exemption:** A real office, qualified staff at market salaries, and strategic decisions taken on Singaporean territory (for pure holding structures the requirements are softer — but a nominee secretary and a registered address are categorically insufficient).

* **The stamp-duty gate on real estate: ABSD 60%:**
  * A foreigner's attempt to buy residential property in Singapore triggers the **Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) of 60%** of the object's value!
  * Buy a $2,000,000 SGD apartment — hand over $1,200,000 SGD to the state as an entry toll (the exception — only citizens and PRs of the US, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, and Liechtenstein under free-trade agreements).

* **The limitation of territoriality (section 10(1) vs 10(25)):**
  * The territorial principle exempts foreign income of individuals — BUT income received through foreign partnerships or within professional activity recognized as Singaporean trade/business falls fully under the progressive income-tax scale up to **24%** (the top rate since 2024).

> 🔧 **Engineer-to-human translation (Bob's Rule for Singapore):**
> * **The essence:** The era of "I'll open a hollow Singapore shell on a nominee and route millions from selling foreign software tax-free" is closed by section 10L. Route money into Singapore without an office and real staff — pay 17%.
> * **Where the mine is buried:** A foreigner's apartment purchase costs +60% tax on top.
> * **The action:** If you're routing capital into Singapore — hold proof of Economic Substance, or don't physically repatriate the funds onto Singaporean accounts (keep them outside the jurisdiction).

###### **3.4. Ireland — the techno-hub with a draconian regime for individuals**

Dublin is the European capital of Google, Apple, and Meta, luring business with a 12.5% corporate tax. But for a founder or an individual investor, Ireland prepares an exquisite financial meat grinder.

* **The de-emissioning expropriator: the 8-year Deemed Disposal (sections 730D/730E TCA 1997):**
  * *Primary sources [L1/L2 Spec]:* the *Taxes Consolidation Act 1997 (TCA 1997)*, Part 26, Chapters 4 & 5, sections 730C–730K; the Revenue Tax and Duty Manual Part 27-01-01.
  * **The breach mechanics:** If an Irish tax resident invests in European ETFs (UCITS), Irish funds, or foreign life-insurance policies, the *Gross Roll-up* regime engages.
  * **The water hammer:** Exactly every **8 years** from the fund's purchase date, a fictitious disposal occurs (**Deemed Disposal**). The taxpayer **MUST pay 41% tax** on the entire accumulated virtual appreciation — even if the fund was never sold and no dividends were paid!
  * **The loss-offset prohibition:** Losses on stocks or other assets **CANNOT** be set against the 41% ETF tax. This is a pure tax on unrealized capital gains, destroying the compound-interest effect.

* **The Close Company Surcharge (sections 440/441 TCA 1997):**
  * *The Level 1 correction:* The draft's single "20% surcharge" must be split into two distinct compositions. **Section 440** levies a 20% surcharge on undistributed *investment* income and income from real estate (*estate and investment income*). For companies providing professional services (IT consulting, engineering, contract software development), a separate norm applies — **section 441 TCA 1997**, charging a 20% surcharge **on 50% of undistributed professional-services income** left after the 12.5% corporate tax, if the profit isn't paid out as dividends within 18 months of the financial year's end.
  * The meaning of the 12.5% corporate tax is nullified: you are forcibly evicted into distribution under income tax up to **52%** (Income Tax 40% + USC 8% + PRSI 4%).

* **The mines of the Non-Domicile regime (section 71 TCA 1997):**
  * Ireland retains a remittance-basis regime for persons without an Irish domicile (Non-Dom). Foreign income isn't taxed until brought into the country.
  * **The trap:** Paying for dinner in Dublin with a foreign credit card, transferring even €100 to an Irish account, or buying a plane ticket from funds where the capital body is mixed with interest/dividends triggers the full toxification of the transfer (*Mixed Funds Rules* — the "income first" presumption) with tax assessed at up to 52%.

> 🔧 **Engineer-to-human translation (Bob's Rule for Ireland):**
> * **The essence:** In Ireland, it's paradise for corporate giants and hell for the private investor. Bought an ordinary S&P 500 index ETF — every 8 years hand over 41% of the unrealized profit straight from your pocket.
> * **Where the mine is buried:** You can't park personal savings inside an Irish holding company — the Close Company surcharge of 20% (s. 440 on investment income, s. 441 on half of undistributed service income) will eat you.
> * **The action:** Living in Ireland — forget buying UCITS ETFs. Invest in individual stocks directly (standard CGT 33% applies to them, with no 8-year fictitious execution) or use pension vehicles (PRSA). Transform the business model from contract development into SaaS / IP licensing — selling software rights doesn't fall under section 441's definition of professional services.

###### **3.5. The Netherlands — the saga of Box 3, the Kerstarrest, and the collapse of the 30% regime**

In the Netherlands, over 90% of the population speaks fluent English. But the Dutch tax system is going through a tectonic crisis.

* **The wealth-tax catastrophe: Box 3 and the Hoge Raad rulings (2021–2024):**
  * *Primary sources [L1/L2 Spec]:* the Supreme Court of the Netherlands (*Hoge Raad*): the epochal *Kerstarrest* of December 24, 2021 (ECLI:NL:HR:2021:1963) and the package of rulings of **June 6, 2024** (ECLI:NL:HR:2024:704) and **August 2, 2024**; the *Overbruggingswet box 3*.
  * **The mechanics of the old trap:** In the Netherlands, assets are taxed in "Box 3." The fisc didn't compute real income — it charged a fictitious return (Forfaitair rendement) of up to 6.04% on investments and took **36% tax** on it (an effective tax on all assets of ~2.17% a year, even if the portfolio fell 30%).
  * **The judicial demolition of the system:** The Supreme Court ruled the system a gross violation of the European Convention on Human Rights (Art. 1 of Protocol 1 — the right to property).
  * **The current trap (2024–2026):** Interim legislation (*Overbruggingswet box 3*) and a rebuttal form (*Opgaaf werkelijk rendement*) were introduced. If your real income is below the fictitious one, you pay on the real one. BUT the Hoge Raad ruled: **real income includes the unrealized appreciation of stocks and crypto**! No expenses other than debt interest are deductible. The full transition to actual-income taxation is deferred to 2027–2028.

* **The dismantling of the 30% ruling (Wet verlaging 30%-regeling):**
  * The famous 30% ruling, which exempted a third of an expat's salary from tax for 5 years, has undergone legislative degradation:
    - From January 1, 2024, a stepped 30-20-10 scheme was introduced (30% for the first 20 months, 20% for the next 20 months, 10% for the last 20 months).
    - Under business pressure (ASML threatened relocation), at the end of 2024 the government revised the terms: from 2027, a permanent flat **27%** benefit rate applies, and the minimum qualifying salary threshold is raised.
  * **The main hidden blow:** From January 1, 2025, the *Partial Non-Resident Tax Status* was **fully liquidated**. Holders of the 30% ruling are no longer exempt from declaring worldwide assets in Box 2 and Box 3! All foreign accounts and stakes in foreign startups now glow in front of the Belastingdienst.

* **The excessive-borrowing law: Wet excessief lenen bij eigen vennootschap:**
  * Holding more than 5% of a Dutch BV or a foreign company (Box 2)?
  * If your aggregate debt to your own company (excluding a mortgage on the main residence) exceeds **€500,000** (the threshold lowered from €700,000 in 2024), the excess is **automatically taxed as a dividend at the Box 2 rate** — **[Level 1 correction:]** up to **31%** in 2025–2026 per the Belastingplan 2025 (a two-tier scale: 24.5% on income up to €67,804 and 31% above; the 33% top rate of 2024 is repealed).

> 🔧 **Engineer-to-human translation (Bob's Rule for the Netherlands):**
> * **The essence:** In Holland the wealth tax is broken: the state took tax for years on a return drawn out of thin air, the court struck it down — and in exchange the tax authority now makes you pay tax on the unrealized profit of your stocks.
> * **Where the mine is buried:** The abolition of Partial Non-Resident Status. Expats with the 30% ruling used to legally shield foreign brokerage accounts. Since 2025, all your assets in the US or the EU are in Box 3's crosshairs.
> * **The action:** Entering the Netherlands — file the *Opgaaf werkelijk rendement* form when markets fall, keep no debt to your BV above €500k, and prepare for the full disclosure of your global portfolio.

###### **3.6. New Zealand — the fiction of the Fair Dividend Rate and the FIF riser**

New Zealand has no classical Capital Gains Tax (CGT), which attracts crypto investors and IT founders. But in place of a CGT there's a hidden hydraulic pump called the **FIF**.

* **The Foreign Investment Fund (FIF) regime — subparts CQ, DN, EX of the Income Tax Act 2007:**
  * *Primary sources [L1/L2 Spec]:* the *Income Tax Act 2007*, sections CQ-5, EX-28–EX-61; the Inland Revenue guide *IR461*.
  * **The breach mechanics:** If a New Zealand tax resident holds stakes in foreign companies, funds, or overseas ETFs (US stocks, European funds) with an aggregate cost above **NZD 50,000** (a September 2026 bill proposes raising the de minimis threshold to $100,000 from April 1, 2027), the FIF regime engages.
  * **The FDR method (Fair Dividend Rate):** By default the law presumes your portfolio generates **5% of fictitious annual return** on the opening-year market value!
  * This attributed 5% income is added to your salary and taxed at the top marginal rate of up to **39%**.
  * **The true price:** A hidden **wealth tax of up to 1.95% annually (5% × 39%)**! The market crashed 40%? You still owe tax on 5% of the opening value (the Comparative Value method lets you lower the base in loss years only with a confirmed market valuation of all closing positions).
  * *The exception:* Most stocks listed on the Australian exchange (ASX) are exempt from FIF under the Trans-Tasman agreement.

* **The buffer for new migrants: the Transitional Resident Exemption (section HR 8 ITA 2007):**
  * New Zealand's only rescue valve: for first-time arriving migrants (or New Zealanders absent for more than 10 years), a **48-month (4-year) tax-free quarantine** applies.
  * For 4 years, foreign passive income, interest, dividends, and FIF calculations are exempt from New Zealand tax. But exactly on day 1461 the valve slams shut, and the entire global portfolio goes on the FDR 5% meter.

* **The Bright-Line Property Test:**
  * Selling residential property before the set term triggers income tax on the entire gain. From July 1, 2024, the coalition government returned the test's term to **2 years** (cutting it from the draconian 10 years introduced by Labour) — but the very fact of a resale tax remains a hard barrier.

> 🔧 **Engineer-to-human translation (Bob's Rule for New Zealand):**
> * **The essence:** New Zealand supposedly "has no capital-gains tax." Yeah — hold out your pocket. In its place works the FIF FDR: hold American stocks or a Vanguard ETF worth over NZD $50k — pay the fisc up to 1.95% of the portfolio's entire value every year, even if the market fell into hell all year.
> * **Where the mine is buried:** The end of the 4-year transitional window. Expats forget that after 4 years their investment portfolio starts drinking live cash.
> * **The action:** While the 4-year *Transitional Resident* status holds — restructure the assets. Either roll into exempt Australian ASX stocks, or move the portfolio into specialized New Zealand PIE funds (Portfolio Investment Entity), where the tax is capped at 28%.

##### **4. The Gap Analysis: the P0/P1 blind zones of the base countries**

###### **4.1. Australia (ATO): the forced residency of foreign structures via Central Management and Control (CMAC)**

* *Primary sources (L1/L2 Spec):* section 6(1) of the *Income Tax Assessment Act 1936*; the High Court of Australia precedent *Bywater Investments Ltd v. Commissioner of Taxation* [2016] HCA 45; Commissioner TR 2018/5; *Practical Compliance Guideline PCG 2018/9*.
* **The physical-legal mechanics of the water hammer:** An IT entrepreneur moves to Australia while keeping an operating micro-corporation in a foreign zero- or low-tax jurisdiction (a Wyoming LLC, a Delaware C-Corp, a UK Ltd, or RAK ICC). The ATO's position, entrenched in TR 2018/5 after the *Bywater Investments* verdict: if the company's key management decisions are made by a person physically located in Australia, the company is automatically recognized an Australian tax resident under the *central management and control test*. The former presumption — which required simultaneous operational trading inside Australia for residency — is annulled. Any regular code commit, commercial-contract negotiation, SaaS invoicing, or banking transaction from an Australian IP address qualifies as the exercise of senior-management functions. The legal entity becomes a full Australian taxpayer from the founder's date of entry.
* **The penalties and financial consequences:** The foreign corporation's entire worldwide revenue is taxed at the Australian corporate rate of 25% (or 30% if qualified as a passive investment structure). Since the foreign company kept no Australian franking account, profit distributions to the founder are taxed as unfranked dividends at income-tax rates up to 47% with no credit — a cumulative effective rate above 60%. In parallel: penalties for failure to lodge corporate returns (sch. 1, s. 286-75 of the *Taxation Administration Act 1953*) and a shortfall penalty of up to 75% for *intentional disregard* (s. 284-75).
* **The engineering preventive valve:** Separating operational function from strategic management. Appointing an independent non-resident board with confirmed industry expertise and real authority to overrule the founder. Holding all board meetings and signing all legally binding documents strictly outside Australia, with minutes and geolocation evidence fixed. The Australian resident in such a structure is engaged exclusively as a technical contractor on fixed compensation, stripped of sole banking signatures and of authority to transact on behalf of the foreign company.

###### **4.2. Canada (CRA): the GAAR modernization of 2024 and the presumption of absent economic substance**

* *Primary sources (L1/L2 Spec):* section 245 of the *Income Tax Act (Canada)* as amended by *Bill C-59* (Royal Assent June 20, 2024); subsections 245(4.1), 245(5.1), 245(5.2); the mandatory transaction-disclosure rules of section 237.3 ITA.
* **The physical-legal mechanics of the water hammer:** Bill C-59 radically transformed Canada's general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR). The threshold for qualifying an "avoidance transaction" in section 245(3) was lowered from the "primary purpose" criterion to **"one of the main purposes."** The key blow is the new subsection 245(4.1): if a transaction or series of transactions is *significantly lacking in economic substance*, the law establishes a direct **presumption of misuse or abuse** of the tax legislation. The indicators of absent substance are named outright: no reasonable likelihood of pre-tax profit relative to the tax benefit, unchanged economic risk between related parties, and a mismatch of legal form to economic substance. Any optimization of service SaaS flows through zero-tax structures without local hiring now automatically falls under GAAR qualification.
* **The penalties and financial consequences:** A new direct penalty under section 245(5.1) of **25% of the tax arising** from the GAAR application (previously the section carried no automatic financial penalty). Under the amended section 152(4)(b)(viii), the normal reassessment period for GAAR-eligible transactions is automatically extended by **3 additional years** unless the transaction was voluntarily disclosed per section 237.3 (Form T661). On the unwinding of an offshore circuit, income re-qualifies as personal income assessed at up to 53.53% (Ontario) plus the 25% GAAR penalty plus CRA compound interest on the arrears.
* **The engineering preventive valve:** Filing a preventive notice under section 237.3(12.1) ITA (*Voluntary Reportable Transaction Disclosure*), which normatively excludes the 25% GAAR penalty and blocks the 3-year extension of the reassessment period. A full rejection of interposed micro-corporations in favor of direct business through a CCPC, with demonstrated economic profitability and commercial purpose for every intercompany agreement.

###### **4.3. Singapore (IRAS): the re-qualification of crypto assets into trading under the "Badges of Trade" doctrine**

* *Primary sources (L1/L2 Spec):* section 10(1)(a) of the *Income Tax Act 1947*; the official IRAS e-Tax Guide *Income Tax Treatment of Digital Tokens* (2024–2026 editions); the judicial doctrine of the *Badges of Trade*.
* **The physical-legal mechanics of the water hammer:** The widespread belief that the Singapore jurisdiction automatically exempts crypto operations because of the absence of a capital-gains tax is a critical legal error. Singapore's tax code contains no general capital-gains exemption: only income of a *capital* rather than *revenue* nature is exempt. IRAS evaluates the transactions of individuals and funds through the objective *Badges of Trade*: the frequency and regularity of operations, short holding periods (under 6 months), systematic profit-taking, the use of trading software or leverage, and active liquidity provision in DeFi protocols (yield farming). On detecting these signs, the activity is re-qualified from passive investing into commercial trading.
* **The penalties and financial consequences:** All profit from crypto operations is retrospectively included in the taxable income of the current period and taxed at the progressive personal income-tax scale of up to **24%**. On qualifying the taxpayer's conduct as an incorrect return without reasonable excuse, section 95(1) ITA imposes a penalty of **100% of the shortfall**; on gross negligence, section 95(2) — **up to 200%**; in cases of willful evasion (section 96) the penalty reaches **300%**, with potential criminal prosecution.
* **The engineering preventive valve:** Strict technical and legal segregation of cold-storage (buy-and-hold) wallets from operational addresses. Drafting investment memoranda at the moment of acquiring base assets, fixing an investment horizon of over two years. Excluding systematic trading activity and liquidity farming from the personal public addresses of a Singapore tax resident. Running active trading operations exclusively through a separate offshore corporate structure, protected from the risks of Singapore permanent-establishment attribution.

###### **4.4. Ireland (Revenue): the Service Company Surcharge (section 441) and the PAYE re-qualification of withdrawals**

* *Primary sources (L1/L2 Spec):* sections 440 and 441 of the *Taxes Consolidation Act 1997*; section 23A TCA 1997 (corporate-residency rules); the Revenue Tax and Duty Manual *Part 13-02-06: Professional Service Company Surcharge*.
* **The physical-legal mechanics of the water hammer:** IT entrepreneurs register an Irish company (LTD) counting on the preferential 12.5% corporate rate. However, Irish fiscal legislation contains a special gate for professional-services companies (*service companies*). If a company is controlled by five or fewer participants (*close company*) and its income derives from professional services (including contract software development, engineering, consulting), **section 441 TCA 1997** applies: a **20% surcharge on 50% of undistributed service income** remaining after the 12.5% corporate tax, if that profit isn't paid to participants as dividends within 18 months of the financial year's end. Retaining service revenue inside the Irish company is thus economically senseless.
  If the founder instead leaves a foreign company (a US LLC / UK Ltd) and manages it from Dublin, the company becomes an Irish tax resident under the management-and-control test, and any payments to the founder re-qualify as director's remuneration with mandatory PAYE withholding at up to **52%** (Income Tax 40% + USC 8% + PRSI 4%).
* **The penalties and financial consequences:** Forced profit extraction to the individual at 52%. On retaining funds in the company, the combined corporate rate with the surcharge reaches **30%**. Penalties for PAYE agent failures under section 1077E TCA run from 10% to 100% of the un-withheld tax, plus public naming in the quarterly *Revenue Defaulters List*.
* **The engineering preventive valve:** Transforming the business model from contract development or IT service consulting into standardized software distribution (SaaS / IP licensing) — the sale of software-use rights does not fall under section 441's definition of professional services. Spreading the shareholding beyond the closed group, or reinvesting undistributed profit into qualifying R&D with the R&D Tax Credit (30% from 2024).

###### **4.5. The Netherlands (Belastingdienst): the Gebruikelijkloonregeling and the protective exit assessment (Conserverende Aanslag)**

* *Primary sources (L1/L2 Spec):* section 12a of the *Wet op de loonbelasting 1964*; sections 4.14, 4.16, 7.5 of the *Wet inkomstenbelasting 2001*; the Belastingdienst circulars on the standard wage.
* **The physical-legal mechanics of the water hammer:** Holding a 5%+ stake in a Dutch BV or a foreign company (Box 2, *aanmerkelijk belang*) obliges the majority director (DGA) to accrue and pay themselves an "customary" market salary (*gebruikelijk loon*) annually. For 2024–2026 the normative minimum is **at least €56,000 a year** (or the salary of the company's highest-paid employee, or the market rate of an equivalent specialist, if higher). The IT entrepreneur has no right to "leave all revenue in the company" or pay themselves Box 2 dividends at 24.5%–31% until the normative salary base is fully closed. That sum is taxed on the progressive Box 1 scale at up to **49.5%**.
  An additional water hammer strikes on relocation out of the Netherlands: the tax authority issues a so-called protective assessment (*conserverende aanslag*). The assessment fixes the unrealized market appreciation of the company's shares and the accumulated profit on the day of departure and taxes it at the Box 2 rate (up to 31%), forbidding the liquidation or alienation of the stake without settling with the Dutch budget.
* **The penalties and financial consequences:** An automatic income-tax assessment at 49.5% on the normative minimum of €56,000 (or more) for every financial year since entry into the country. A penalty for understating the salary base (*verzuimboete / vergrijpboete*) of up to 100% of the arrears, plus punitive interest (*belastingrente*) at 7.5% a year. Corporate restructuring is blocked by the security requirements of the protective assessment.
* **The engineering preventive valve:** Filing a formal request with the Belastingdienst to approve a reduced normative salary (*verzoek verlaging gebruikelijk loon*) with an auditor's report on the early-stage startup's cash flow. Using the state WBSO subsidy for innovative software development, which lowers the company's wage-tax obligations on the founder's salary. Capitalizing and fixing the stake's valuation before entering the Netherlands.

###### **4.6. New Zealand (IRD): the disqualification of the tax-free crypto status under section CB 4**

* *Primary sources (L1/L2 Spec):* sections CB 4 and CB 3 of the *Income Tax Act 2007*; the Inland Revenue technical decision *TDS 25/23*; the directive public document *IRRUIP18: Cryptoassets and the purpose of disposal test*.
* **The physical-legal mechanics of the water hammer:** New Zealand has no separate capital-gains law, which breeds the expat illusion of a tax-free crypto hub. But the tax statute contains **section CB 4 ITA 2007**: any amount received from disposing of *personal property* is taxable income if the property was acquired **with the purpose of subsequent sale or disposal**. Per the IRD's position, confirmed by *TDS 25/23*, cryptocurrencies generate no regular dividend income and grant no rights of material possession (unlike commercial real estate or dividend stocks), so the IRD legislatively **presumes** that the dominant purpose of acquiring digital assets is their subsequent disposal at a profit. The burden of rebutting this presumption (*onus of proof*) rests entirely on the taxpayer. Any sale of crypto into fiat, any token-to-token swap (crypto-to-crypto), or payment for goods is taxable income.
* **The penalties and financial consequences:** All gross profit from digital-asset disposals is added to the individual's income and taxed at the top personal rate of **39%**. Losses from failed positions don't automatically offset other active-income categories unless the activity is recognized as a full business. On audit: shortfall penalties per the *Tax Administration Act 1994* — 20% for *lack of reasonable care*, 40% for *gross carelessness*, 150% for *evasion* — plus UOMI (*Use of Money Interest*) of over 10% a year.
* **The engineering preventive valve:** Entering New Zealand exclusively under the protection of the 4-year *Transitional Resident* exemption (section HR 8 ITA 2007), which shields foreign passive capital and crypto assets acquired before the move from IRD jurisdiction. Fixing positions and withdrawing funds before the 48-month transitional period expires. Permanent tax residents are prescribed to invest in crypto via New Zealand institutional PIE funds (*Portfolio Investment Entity*), where the top rate is fixed at 28%.

##### **5. The Expansion into the Adjacent "High-English" Jurisdictions**

###### **5.1. The UK (post-2025 reform): the demolition of Non-Dom and the 4-year FIG regime**

* *The regime and its statutory base:* The liquidation of the domicile institution and remittance-basis taxation; the introduction of the *Foreign Income and Gains (FIG)* regime; *Finance Act 2025* (in force from April 6, 2025); the inheritance-tax (IHT) reform.
* **The anatomy of the trap for the solo founder/expat:** The reform liquidated the century-old institution of the indefinite Non-Dom status. The new FIG regime grants temporary exemption from UK taxation of foreign income and capital gains strictly for the **first 4 tax years** of residency. To qualify, the applicant must have been a UK tax non-resident for **at least 10 consecutive tax years** before entry. Electing FIG strips the taxpayer of the standard Personal Allowance (£12,570) and the CGT Annual Exempt Amount.
  The main trap is the year-5 transition: the individual is automatically moved onto the worldwide *arising basis*, with all income taxed at up to **45%** and capital gains at up to **24%**.
  Furthermore, a residence-based IHT model was introduced: a person who lived in the UK for **10 of the last 20 years** has their entire worldwide estate subject to the 40% inheritance tax — with the status persisting 3 to 10 years after final departure (the *tail provision*). Excluded-property protection for offshore trusts, for new structures, is fully annulled.
* **The comparative analysis:** In its 4-year nature, FIG mirrors New Zealand's *Transitional Resident* (section HR 8). But the UK is markedly inferior to New Zealand: it carries a capital-gains tax (up to 24%), hard CFC rules, and the 40% death-tax risk.

###### **5.2. Hong Kong: the Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) 2023–2026**

   * *No elevators* (shaft lockup and guide-cable snapping).

##### **11.7.7. Active-shooter incidents in enclosed spaces: the Run, Hide, Fight doctrine ([P0-69: ACTIVE-SHOOTER-PROTOCOL])**

On a sudden armed attack in public buildings, offices, or shopping centers, the passive freeze response ("sit and wait for the police") guarantees maximal losses. The only proven survivable model — the **"Run, Hide, Fight" doctrine (FBI / DHS / ALERRT)**:

```
                  [ AN ACTIVE SHOOTER IN THE BUILDING ]
                                         │
        ┌────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                ▼                                ▼
   [ 1. RUN ]                     [ 2. HIDE ]                     [ 3. FIGHT ]
A verified clear path           The path is cut: barricade      Point-blank contact: attack
Drop belongings, hands open     Locks, belts on door closers    Group fury, improvised
Run in zigzag to the exit       Lights off, phone SILENCED      weapon grabs
```

1. **RUN (Priority #1):**
   * If shots are heard and a verified evacuation route exists — run immediately.
   * **Drop all belongings:** No packing backpacks, laptops, or jackets.
   * Don't try to evacuate resisting panickers — leave yourself.
   * Exiting to the street, keep **palms open, empty, and raised to shoulder level**, so police assault teams don't mistake you for a threat.
2. **HIDE (if the path is cut):**
   * Retreat to a blind isolated room outside the shooter's line of sight;
   * **Hardware door blocking:** Lock the deadbolt, pile heavy furniture (tables, cabinets, safes). Stretch belts or cables over the hinges and hydraulic closers to exclude the door opening outward;
   * **Light and acoustic blackout:** Kill 100% of lights and monitors, draw the curtains. Set smartphones to **absolute silence (no vibration!)**;
   * Disperse along blind load-bearing walls, not clustering at the door.
3. **FIGHT (only as the last line):**
   * Applied **EXCLUSIVELY on the shooter's direct breach into the room** and imminent execution.
   * **Total aggression and the door ambush:** Attack from the doorframe's blind zone at the moment of the shooter's entry.
   * **Heavy improvised weapons:** Fire extinguishers (foam/powder to the face blinds and chokes the shooter; the body serves as a striking projectile), monitors, chair legs, scissors.
   * **A group attack:** Seizing and pinning the weapon barrel downward, destabilizing the shooter with strikes to eyes/groin/throat, pinning to the floor until SWAT arrives.
